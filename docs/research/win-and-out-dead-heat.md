# Win-and-Out: a dead heat on a bell lap

Research done 2026-Oct-19, before the engine, the register, and the help were brought into line. The readings below are as they stood that evening.

The question: in a Win-and-Out (Danish: the first bell lap wins 1st; Belgian: the first bell lap wins the last place and the last lap decides 1st), what happens when two riders dead-heat a bell lap? The app's documents disagree three ways:

- A. The engine (`madtracker/scoring/win_and_out.py`) and the rule register note on R-WIN-1 (`docs/scoring-rules.md`): the riders share the place and the next bell lap still decides the next place, so the numbering runs 1, 1, 2 (test `test_dead_heat_on_a_place_lap_shares_that_place`: places=3, a dead heat on lap 1, and the first finisher is 4th behind four placed riders).
- B. R-TIE-1 (USAC 1M6(a)) and the help's general line ("A dead heat shares the place, and the next place is skipped: 1, 2, 2, 4"), and R-WIN-5 for the Win-and-Out's own finish fill: an equal placing uses up the next place.
- C. The help's Win-and-Out section: "A dead heat on a bell lap is the officials' call: the place goes to the rider they name."

## What the sources add up to

No club page says a word about a dead heat on a bell lap. Seven club descriptions of the format (Northbrook, Kenosha, San Diego, Dick Lane, Burnaby, Jerry Baker, Victoria) all describe it the same way: one rider wins each bell lap and leaves; the rest fill the places behind. None of the federation texts (UCI Part 3 2026, AusCycling 2025, British Cycling, USAC 8•25) has the format at all. So there is no published Win-and-Out tie rule, and the app's R-WIN-1 note is right about that.

What the texts do give is two general things:

1. A tie on a line is the judges' failure to separate, not a race outcome in its own right. USAC 1M6(a) and 1M4(b) assume one, and 2E3(d)(iii) tells the officials to decide and announce before the back straight, and if they can't, to let nobody out that lap and go again. The rear wheel (elimination) and front wheel (sprint) wording, the photo finish, and the "velodrome officials have the final say" line on Kenosha all point the same way: in practice the officials name one rider.
2. When the officials do declare riders equal, the rulebook's general numbering applies. 1M4(b) says the tied riders are "declared equal and the prizes for those places shall be added and equally divided": "those places", plural, so a pair tied for 2nd occupies 2nd and 3rd and the next rider is 4th. That is the 1, 2, 2, 4 reading the app already uses for every finish order (R-TIE-1), for sprints (2M2(f)(i), R-POINTS-2), and for the Win-and-Out's own finish fill (R-WIN-5). 1M4(b) also says a dead heat for 1st is re-ridden, not shared.

## The three readings against those facts

Reading A (engine today, 1, 1, 2): the dead heat uses one place number for two riders, so with places=3 four riders carry the numbers 1 to 3 and the first finisher is 4th with four riders ahead. That is a tabulation the rulebook never produces: every other place in the app, and every USAC result, counts riders ahead. It also contradicts R-WIN-5 inside the same race (a tie in the fill skips, a tie on a lap does not), and it would feed a Team Omnium by Place table and the USAC CSV a "2nd" with two riders ahead. The note's reason, "that's what the bell told the riders", is weaker than it looks: the bell only says "sprint lap"; the place the lap is for is announced, and the officials announce the next lap after they rule on the dead heat, so they can and will say "this lap is for 4th".

Reading B (1M6(a) as read everywhere else, 1, 1, 3): consistent with the rest of the app and with the rulebook's prize wording. Under a USAC sanction the general rules govern what the club description leaves open, and 1M4(b) and 1M6(a) are the general rules. In the Belgian direction the same arithmetic moves the tied pair up: two riders tied for the 5th-place lap occupy 4th and 5th jointly and both are 4th, and the next bell is for 3rd. In either direction a dead heat uses up two places, so the race has one fewer bell lap than Places; the officials need to know that, and the app's task line can say which place the next lap is for.

Reading C (the officials name one rider): true as a description of what happens at the line, and the app should say it, but it is not a scoring rule. The entry model lets the scorer record a tie on a lap, and the engine has to number it somehow; C alone leaves that open. C also sits oddly beside a dead heat for 1st, where 1M4(b) says re-ride rather than "name one".

## Recommendation

Adopt B, and say C in the help as the usual case. The app numbers a declared dead heat on a bell lap the way it numbers every other equal placing (1M6(a), 1M4(b)): the tied riders share the place, the next place is skipped, and the next bell lap is for the next open place. A dead heat for 1st (the first bell lap of a Danish race, the last of a Belgian) is re-ridden under 1M4(b): the officials ring the bell again for the same place, and the scorer records the winner once named (the app needs no new entry for this; the lap is recorded when it is decided). The engine's 1, 1, 2 numbering, the R-WIN-1 note, and the two tests that pin it (`test_dead_heat_on_a_place_lap_shares_that_place`, `test_dnf_group_follows_the_last_announced_place`) would change to 1, 1, 3 and a DNF group after the last used place, and R-WIN-1's register entry would cite 1M6(a) and 1M4(b) instead of "the program's choice". The maintainer decides whether the task line should name the place the next bell is for after a tie.

One sentence for the help page:

"The officials name one rider per bell lap (rear wheel in an Elimination, front wheel in a sprint, or the photo); if they declare a dead heat, both riders take the place and the next place is skipped, as in any other finish (1M6(a)), and the next bell lap is for the next open place, except that a dead heat for 1st is re-ridden (1M4(b))."

Shorter, if the help keeps the general tie line nearby:

"A dead heat on a bell lap that the officials declare shares the place and skips the next one (1M6(a)); the next bell lap is for the next open place, and a dead heat for 1st is re-ridden (1M4(b))."
